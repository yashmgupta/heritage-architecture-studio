# Architectural research and responsible descriptions

These models are **inspired form studies**, not measured reproductions. Architectural types span different periods, materials, cultures, and construction traditions. The title retains “Ancient” as an established project name; the catalogue should be described as **ancient and historic architecture**, because some references are medieval.

- **Maya stepped temple:** Chichén Itzá-inspired geometry, with terraces, axial stairways and a summit shrine. No claim of exact reconstruction, calendar encoding or equinox simulation.
- **Khmer sanctuary:** Angkor-inspired concentric terraces and five-tower composition. Real monuments also include extensive galleries, waterways, enclosures and reliefs not represented in a compact model.
- **Buddhist stupa:** Sanchi-inspired solid mound, harmika, parasol and circumambulatory layout. Do not characterize it as a hollow walk-in temple.
- **Timber pagoda:** Yingxian-inspired timber construction with projecting roofs; masonry/block counts do not represent timber material usage. The historical pagoda dates to the medieval period.
- **Roman aqueduct:** Semicircular arches, piers and a top water channel inspired by surviving aqueducts. No hydraulic performance or structural thrust calculation.
- **Egyptian/step pyramids, classical temples, and Indian temple traditions:** simplified geometry should be qualified as a study of massing and proportion, not archaeologically verified engineering drawings.

## Recommended institutional starting points

These external organizations provide useful context. Links are included for research, not as proof that any modeled proportions are exact:

- [UNESCO: Chichén Itzá](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/483/)
- [APSARA National Authority](https://apsaraauthority.gov.kh/)
- [UNESCO: Buddhist Monuments at Sanchi](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/524/)
- [UNESCO: Chinese Wooden Pagoda of Yingxian (Tentative List)](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentative-lists/5314/)
- [Pont du Gard](https://pontdugard.fr/)

When contributing new references, prefer official conservation authorities and primary scholarly literature, cite an exact page/record when possible, distinguish a contemporary interpretation from a surveyed reconstruction, and avoid treating any one monument as representative of a whole culture.
